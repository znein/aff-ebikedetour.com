How it feels

Torque sensor vs cadence sensor

The sensor decides how the motor responds when you pedal. It changes how an e-bike feels more than any number on the spec sheet does.

We earn a commission if you buy through our links. It doesn't change the price you pay.

Cadence sensor: on or off

A cadence sensor detects that the pedals are turning. Once they turn, the motor delivers a set amount of power for the assist level you've chosen, however hard you push. It feels like a switch: easy, relaxed cruising, but the bike can surge as you start pedaling and keep pushing when you'd rather ease off.

Kingbull bikes with cadence sensors: Ranger, Hunter 2.0S, Hunter 2.0, Literider 2.0, Expedition.

Torque sensor: more push, more help

A torque sensor measures how hard you press on the pedals and adds power in proportion. Push hard on a hill and the motor works hard; pedal gently and it helps gently. It feels like a normal bike with stronger legs, and it usually uses the battery more efficiently because power goes where you need it.

Kingbull bikes with torque sensors: Discover 2.0, Discover ST 2.0, Voyager 2.0, Trekker, Verve, Jumper Go, Rover 2.0, Dual Battery Voyager 2.0, EX Titan.

Which should you get?

  • Mostly throttle, or relaxed cruising: cadence is fine and the bikes cost less. The Ranger ($749) and Hunter 2.0S ($949) are the value picks.
  • Commuting, fitness or technical trails: a torque sensor is worth paying for. The cheapest Kingbull with one is $1,199 (Discover 2.0, Discover ST 2.0, Voyager 2.0, Trekker, Verve).
  • Riding with traffic or on crowded paths: torque sensors give finer control at low speed.

Every Kingbull also has a throttle, so either type can be ridden without pedaling (up to 20 mph on most models). Compare all bikes.
